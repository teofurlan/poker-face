03 · Deadpan

The funniest room is the quietest one. Poker Face becomes a very serious gallery: bone-coloured wall, black ink, a large literary serif, museum-label captions in italic. The hot seat is a portrait waiting to be hung; the crack counter is a hand-kept tally; the only vermilion in the whole product is a crack, and when one lands a single hairline fracture runs across the page. Everything else holds perfectly still, so the room can watch the face instead of the screen.

Tradeoff: the most distinctive and the most readable in a lit room or on a TV in daylight; the quietest, so it relies on the players to supply the noise. Less arcade energy than the other two, and the wittiest copy depends on staying deadpan.

Palette
bone wall paper ink graphite vermilion (cracks only)
Type
Palatino Linotype serif for headings, names and numerals; Segoe UI only for small museum-label captions. Ship a self-hosted text serif with true lining figures.
Motion
Almost none. A crack draws one vermilion hairline across the page in half a second and the counter's numeral inks in; the mood bead drifts along its line. Nothing else moves.
Sound
Quiet room tone with soft felt ticks for the countdown; a small ceramic "tink" on a crack; a single low gallery bell for the winner.
Roll
Grounded candidate from the audience's world (gallery and museum placards).
