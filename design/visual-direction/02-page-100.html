Poker Face as a teletext page: pure black, seven flat colours, a fixed 40 × 20 character grid, and nothing curved. Players are lines on a page, the countdown is a subpage number, and the bottom of every screen carries the four colour keys, so the buttons are always where the room expects them. The mosaic "straight face" is the game's one character: its mouth is a flat bar and bends into a smile the instant a crack lands.

Tradeoff: the highest contrast and the most readable from across the room, with a witty, nostalgic voice that suits a party of mixed ages; hardest to extend (everything must snap to the grid) and the least "premium". Motion is deliberately cheap and instant.

Palette
black white yellow cyan green magenta red blue (fills only)
Type
One monospace on a 32 × 40 px cell (Cascadia Mono here; ship a self-hosted pixel/teletext face). Double-height and double-size rows for headings; block digits built from cells.
Motion
Hard swaps only, locked to the cell grid. A crack flips the whole band to red and bends the mosaic mouth; one 1 Hz steady/flash cell blink; no easing anywhere.
Sound
Square-wave bleeps: one per countdown subpage, a two-note drop plus a quick arpeggio on a crack, a click on each colour key.
Roll
Challenger from the dice (teletext weather mosaic), fused with the product.
